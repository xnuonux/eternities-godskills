# Universal specialist refinement

This report records the Lane B refinement of 28 product skill directories in the universal product pack. The 22 operational entries were rewritten as portable implementation-and-evidence guides, the two candidate entries were included as instruction-reviewed under their existing IDs, and four first-party specialist entries were added. No performance qualification or certification occurred. Each entry has a frontmatter `name` and useful `description`, a schema-conforming `skill.json`, instruction-reviewed maturity, relative resource metadata, stable IDs, and repository-relative provenance. The historical `skills/` sources remain separate and were not rewritten.

This is an independent authoring pass. Source repositories, commits, and relative source locations are recorded in each manifest; no upstream skill prose is reproduced and no source result is presented as an agent-performance result.

The category synthesis and its exact bounded source paths were read before authoring. The four new specialists use these source methods:

## Per-skill changes

- `api-rate-limit-recovery`: Reframed recovery around observable response headers, retry budgets, jittered backoff, idempotency, cancellation, and bounded escalation. Receipts now distinguish a simulated retry from a live provider response and preserve the reason a retry was or was not safe.
- `approval-bound-private-session-mining`: Added an approval ledger, explicit session and artifact scope, private-source boundary, redaction, least-collection rules, and evidence indexing. The workflow can implement an authorized extraction while refusing to treat discoverability or a partial scrape as permission.
- `bounded-service-shutdown`: Made shutdown a state machine: stop admission, quiesce, drain, bound the wait, escalate, and verify the terminal state. It now covers signals, child processes, repeated invocation, stuck work, durable receipts, and recovery without claiming that a requested stop succeeded before observation.
- `bounded-verified-object-ingestion`: Added object identity and content-hash checks, schema and metadata validation, atomic staging, quarantine, replay, deduplication, and post-commit verification. It keeps source acquisition, transformation, indexing, and publication as separate evidence steps.
- `columnar-ingestion-rollup-and-query-layout-design`: Added workload-first layout selection, event-time and late-arrival handling, partition and clustering tradeoffs, rollup freshness, compaction, schema evolution, and query-plan evidence. Recommendations now require representative measurements and preserve a migration/rollback path.
- `confirmed-destructive-reconstruction`: Converted recovery into a manifest-driven, dry-run-first reconstruction with explicit destructive scope, confirmation, snapshot/hash checks, dependency order, idempotence, and rollback or quarantine. It records what was inspected, what changed, and what could not be reconstructed.
- `diagnostic-statistical-model-inference`: Added estimand and contrast definition, confounder review, model alternatives, residual and sensitivity checks, uncertainty, negative findings, and falsifiable next measurements. It separates exploratory inference from a causal or production decision claim.
- `docx-package-redline-and-render-verification`: Added package-level checks for relationships, content types, tracked-change structure, comments, numbering, and media, followed by clean-output rendering and visual review. It retains source/output hashes and distinguishes XML validity from reader rendering.
- `formula-preserving-workbook-engineering`: Added formula, style, merge, defined-name, table, sheet-order, and external-link preservation checks, plus recalculation and rendered-sheet review. The workflow verifies both formula text and evaluated values where available and flags unsupported calculation engines.
- `fp-ts-functional-refactoring`: Recast refactoring around type-level and runtime behavior contracts: algebraic data types, error channels, effect boundaries, combinator laws, and import/API compatibility. It requires focused typecheck, lint, tests, semantic diff, and explicit handling of generated or unused code.
- `genomic-coordinate-assembly-and-variant-gates`: Added assembly and contig identity, coordinate-base and interval semantics, strand normalization, liftover/variant representation, reference checks, duplicate/overlap handling, and gateable invalid cases. Outputs retain provenance and do not turn a coordinate transform into biological interpretation.
- `interface-localization-and-bidirectionality`: Added locale inventory, fallback policy, plural and number/date rules, expansion stress, RTL and bidirectional text hazards, mirroring exceptions, truncation, font coverage, and screenshot/accessibility checks. It keeps translation completeness distinct from visual and interaction verification.
- `invariant-guard`: Made invariants explicit at boundaries and after mutation, with precondition/postcondition checks, negative cases, property-style generation, clear failure context, and recovery behavior. It discourages silent repair and records which invariant was observed versus merely declared.
- `lazy-tabular-transformation-and-validation`: Added lazy plan construction, predicate/projection pushdown, chunk and memory limits, schema/null semantics, stable ordering, materialization points, and validation of empty, malformed, and duplicate data. It compares plan intent with the materialized result rather than assuming laziness is preserved.
- `measured-paid-creative-iteration`: Added a baseline, one-change creative loop, spend/time/cost ceiling, audience and attribution definition, primary and guardrail metrics, stop rules, frequency/fatigue checks, and rollback. It distinguishes a prepared variant from a paid delivery and a directional signal from a validated increment.
- `performance-release-gating`: Added explicit budgets, representative workloads, cold/warm and concurrency conditions, p50/p95 or equivalent distributions, resource ceilings, regression thresholds, and environment metadata. The release decision now identifies synthetic coverage gaps and refuses to equate a benchmark fixture with production performance.
- `physics-constrained-numerical-validation`: Added unit and frame declarations, equations and constraints, conservation or monotonicity checks, boundary and degenerate cases, solver convergence, tolerances, sensitivity, and independent numeric spot checks. It keeps a plausible plot or stable solver run separate from validated physical meaning.
- `release-script-safety`: Added argument and path validation, preflight, dry-run, idempotency, scoped permissions, exit-code handling, output redaction, cleanup, rollback, and repeat-run checks. Destructive actions require an explicit bounded target and the receipt records the exact mode without using broad machine paths.
- `semantic-implementation-diff`: Added intent-to-change mapping across AST/type/API/behavior layers, generated/vendor filtering, dependency and contract impact, focused execution evidence, and uncertainty reporting. It treats textual similarity as a clue, not semantic equivalence, and preserves a reviewer-readable diff summary.
- `symbolic-mathematics-python`: Added domain, assumptions, exact-versus-floating representation, simplification/equivalence checks, singularity and branch analysis, solver conditions, and numeric spot checks. It reports unresolved assumptions and does not elevate a symbolic expression into a theorem or scientific result without the needed proof boundary.
- `venture-falsification-and-planning`: Added problem and customer evidence, falsifiable risks, cohort and channel definitions, constraint map, unit-economics sensitivity, milestone gates, kill criteria, and decision receipts. It supports an authorized experiment or implementation plan while keeping narrative plausibility separate from validation.
- `web-performance-optimization`: Added lab/field distinction, trace and build capture, Core Web Vitals-style metric definitions, route and device conditions, bundle/cache/image/font analysis, regression budgets, and remeasurement. It identifies attribution limits and does not present a single synthetic run as field performance.
- `audio-dsp-integrity-review`: Included the candidate ID as instruction-reviewed with an independent integrity workflow for sample rate/format/channel layout, latency and block size, phase/impulse/noise response, clipping, deterministic renders, and artifact review. It preserves the boundary between numerical signal checks, listening review, and hardware or perceptual claims.

It supports real authorized CAD iteration without treating a valid mesh or fixture as manufactured evidence.

Seeds, joint limits, collision cases, failure recovery, calibration, and safety/run scope remain tier-specific; authored fixtures are not agent-performance evidence.

Component and fixture results stay below emulator or physical-device claims.

Prepared packets, competitor observations, and synthetic fixtures are not reported as published or validated growth results.

## Metadata and integration notes

All 28 manifests use the compact `taskTypes` vocabulary only: `research`, `plan`, `build`, `verify`, `recover`, `orchestrate`, `create`, and `communicate`. Relationships use stable broad or product IDs; category labels are routing guidance rather than a mandatory taxonomy. Resources are currently empty arrays, so there are no dangling relative links or copied source assets to validate. The four new specialists use optional routing relationships to engineering, safety, release, interface, discovery, and measurement IDs without requiring a companion skill.

The requested 28 entries coexist with 26 pre-existing product directories in this checkout. A fresh inventory therefore reports 54 product directories, not the planned 50; because those other directories were outside this lane's write scope, this report does not remove or edit them. The parent integration owner should reconcile that four-directory inventory gap. This lane added and refined only the 28 requested directories and this report; it did not edit broad skill directories, builder/runtime tests, bulk corpus material, global settings, warehouse state, or external systems.

## Limitations and concrete omissions

- No live agent exercises, provider applies, mobile emulator/device runs, robot/HIL/field runs, store publication, paid creative delivery, or external API execution were performed. The entries therefore remain `instruction-reviewed`, not performance-qualified or certified.
- Parent-owned builder/runtime tests were not edited or regenerated. A scoped structural audit can check manifest shape, finite task labels, relative resources, source-path hygiene, frontmatter, and directory completeness; it cannot substitute for the parent’s runtime validation.
- No source repository content was copied into product resources. This keeps provenance inspectable and avoids implying that cold, inert, or fixture-only source material is a product result, but it leaves each specialist without bundled domain references beyond its rewritten instructions.
- The web-quality accessibility and game level-design source methods named by the intake were reviewed but are not new product IDs in this Lane B scope. Accessibility concerns appear only where they directly support the requested localization/mobile entries; a dedicated accessibility or level-design specialist would require separate authorization.
- Marketplace ranking, infrastructure-provider behavior, native platform behavior, robot safety, audio perceptual quality, spreadsheet recalculation, document-reader rendering, and numerical/biological validity remain environment- and evidence-dependent. The skills provide gates and reporting language, not guarantees.
- Parent integration may still reveal naming, category, relationship, or resource-link policy changes when the complete 50-ID pack is assembled. Those changes are outside this bounded lane and should be made by the owner of the integration contract.
