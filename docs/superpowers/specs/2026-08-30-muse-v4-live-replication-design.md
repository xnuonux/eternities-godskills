# Muse v4 live replication design

## outcome

Create an inactive, task-sliced Muse v4 candidate that improves a strong raw agent rather than replacing its judgment. Test it against raw `gpt-5.6-terra` on three fresh Three.js missions under blinded, matched conditions. The candidate may become adoption-eligible only after the user chooses the refined condition in at least two of three blind comparisons, every hard gate passes, and the added context and wall-time cost remain bounded.

## development evidence

The white-fire paired trial at `C:\dev\white-fire-trial` is development evidence, not held-out proof.

- the user correctly identified A as Godagent and B as raw before reveal;
- raw B won the blind product preference because it preserved true orbit inspection, dimensional altar construction, and less regular secondary motion;
- Godagent A led the formal visual rubric through stronger flame anatomy, composition, and art direction;
- the current Muse package cost 2.73 times the raw prompt bytes and 44 percent more wall time;
- exact reviewed evidence is sealed by `evidence/results-manifest.json`, `evidence/user-verdict.json`, and `evidence/evaluation.md` in the trial repository.

The candidate addresses four failure codes:

1. `spatial-affordance-regression`: visual direction must not remove requested direct manipulation, orbit, zoom, traversal, or inspectability.
2. `natural-motion-regularization`: natural phenomena must be checked for visible spirals, lattices, synchronized pulses, uniform lifetimes, and other accidental order.
3. `solution-space-collapse`: method guidance should define acceptance and failure criteria while leaving rendering technique choices to the agent.
4. `method-cost-overrun`: only the selected task slice should enter model context, with no full Muse contract or unrelated route bodies.

## candidate boundary

Muse v4 remains an inactive experiment under `artifacts/muse-v4-experimental`. It does not replace `skills/eternities-muse`, alter a routing card, change a profile, or rewrite any historical receipt.

The candidate package contains one compact real-time phenomena slice:

- preserve every explicit user affordance before choosing composition;
- protect spatial validity across front, side, near, and far inspection;
- use multi-scale, non-synchronized variation for natural motion and test at multiple times;
- preserve internal luminance detail before adding bloom;
- define observable rejection checks while leaving shaders, particles, geometry, textures, and pass architecture open;
- retain measured performance, accessibility, resize, pause, and lower-tier behavior;
- terminate after one implementation and verification pass under the shared time budget.

The complete injected supplement, including receipt, must be no more than 8,000 UTF-8 bytes and no more than 2,000 estimated tokens. It cannot expand authority.

## held-out missions

Mission identities and wording are frozen before candidate execution.

### gravitational portal

Build an explorable ruined-observatory scene centered on a living gravitational portal. The portal should possess turbulent luminous matter, deep parallax, gravitational lensing or scene distortion, debris, atmospheric response, a dimensional architectural anchor, direct orbit inspection, and a meaningful lower-quality mode.

### captive storm

Build an explorable containment chamber holding an electrically active storm cell. The cloud should roll and fold volumetrically, produce branching lightning and rain or mist, illuminate and shadow the chamber, remain convincing from multiple angles, expose direct orbit inspection, and degrade gracefully.

### bioluminescent ecosystem

Build an explorable alien reef-grove ecosystem with several living organism families. Motion should feel coupled but unsynchronized across fronds, polyps, spores, swimmers, and ambient currents. Light should propagate through nearby forms and haze, the habitat should remain dimensional under direct orbit inspection, and a lower-quality mode must preserve the experience.

## matched conditions

Each mission has two fresh subjects:

- raw Terra receives the frozen mission, neutral scaffold, and development tools only;
- candidate Terra receives the same inputs plus the exact inactive Muse v4 package;
- both use `gpt-5.6-terra`, high reasoning, one invocation, a 30-minute ceiling, isolated Codex homes, independent Git ceilings, and no global AGENTS body, keel, memory, installed skills, sibling path, or prior conversation;
- pairs start concurrently; missions run sequentially to avoid six-way local resource contention;
- the coordinator never edits a submission after launch.

## hard gates

Every submission must build, render a non-empty 2560 by 1440 hardware canvas, produce no page or console errors, survive resize, expose a meaningful quality fallback, and preserve the mission's direct spatial interaction. Any isolation violation or failed hard gate makes that submission ineligible.

## evaluation

Each pair is randomized independently as A/B and presented through a local HTML viewer that loads one candidate at a time. The user chooses the stronger complete product before reveal.

Shared 100-point rubric:

| dimension | points |
| --- | ---: |
| phenomenon realism and internal structure | 20 |
| natural temporal behavior and secondary effects | 15 |
| dimensionality and spatial validity | 15 |
| interaction and affordance preservation | 10 |
| composition and emotional force | 10 |
| technical graphics sophistication | 10 |
| lighting and environmental integration | 10 |
| performance and graceful degradation | 5 |
| evidence honesty and browser stability | 5 |

## adoption gate

The candidate becomes `eligible`, never automatically adopted, only when:

- it wins at least two of three blind user preferences;
- it loses no hard gate and causes no critical rubric regression;
- spatial affordances pass in all three missions;
- natural-motion regularity is not worse than raw in any mission;
- its prompt supplement stays within 8,000 bytes and 2,000 estimated tokens;
- median elapsed-time overhead is at most 35 percent, unless the user explicitly accepts a larger cost for a clearly preferred artifact.

Anything weaker leaves Muse v4 experimental and records the failed gate.
