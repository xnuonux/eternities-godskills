# Agricultural observation and trial

Use this workflow to make a crop, garden or soil decision more evidence-based. It organizes observations and small trials; it does not prescribe universal fertilizer, pesticide, livestock, machinery or chemical treatment instructions.

## Identify the decision and setting

Record crop or plant identity, growth stage, location and season, management history, area and units, soil characteristics, water conditions, recent weather, and the change the user is considering. Preserve what is observed separately from explanations.

For a laboratory report, record sampling date, depth, sample preparation, extraction method, reporting units and the lab's crop- and region-specific interpretation. Values from different methods or depths may not be comparable. Do not import a numeric threshold from an unrelated guide.

## Investigate before changing inputs

1. Map the pattern: uniform or patchy, associated with traffic, slope, irrigation, variety, timing or a previous application. Compare an affected area with a reasonably matched unaffected area.
2. List plausible causes and the observation that could distinguish each. Consider interacting constraints instead of explaining every symptom with one preferred soil theory.
3. Check data quality: sample labeling, collection method, units, instrument calibration, photographs and repeat observations. A single noisy reading is not a trend.
4. Consult locally applicable extension guidance, laboratory recommendations and product labels when a treatment decision requires them. Verify current restrictions and conditions. If important context is unavailable, propose measurement or qualified local review rather than guess an application rate.
5. Prefer a small reversible trial when uncertainty remains and a trial is appropriate. Keep sensitive areas, water, people, animals and neighboring crops outside an unassessed intervention.

## Design a useful comparison

Define the question, comparison, primary outcome, observation dates, treatment identity, area, and stopping condition before beginning. Keep a control where feasible. Replicate or randomize when practical and note spatial and seasonal confounders; an unreplicated before/after observation supports a weaker conclusion.

Measure an outcome relevant to the decision, such as emergence, yield, symptoms, labor or cost, with a consistent method. Record what actually happened, including deviations, weather and adverse effects. Do not erase disappointing trials from the record.

## Deliver and learn

Return a field observation sheet, competing hypotheses, missing measurements, a bounded trial plan if appropriate, and an explicit decision rule. A useful row is: plot, date, condition, observation, units, method, action, outcome and source.

Review the outcome against the predeclared comparison and distinguish local findings from general recommendations. Biological explanations and historical authorities are not substitutes for evidence that a particular intervention will help this crop under these conditions. Finish with the best-supported next decision, uncertainty and a follow-up measurement—not a guaranteed agronomic result.
