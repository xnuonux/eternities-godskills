# Teaching continuity and evidence-linked reports

## Handoff to an unfamiliar facilitator

Make the existing teaching plan usable without its author's memory, private account or usual equipment. Preserve the intended learning outcome; simplify the delivery, not the intellectual goal.

Use the supplied date, timetable, locations, materials, support contacts and authorized routines. For each teaching block, give learner-facing instructions, a workable time budget, one check for understanding, a low-resource alternative, and where completed work belongs. Include confirmed non-teaching duties and a short record-back prompt: what was covered, evidence collected and follow-up needed.

Distinguish a confirmed absence from missing information. An empty medical-alert field does not establish that there are no alerts. Do not invent a local emergency number, medication instruction, support plan, room or contact. Point to the institution's verified procedures and authorized personnel; mark missing safety-critical details for confirmation before the handoff is used. A useful provisional lesson can still be delivered without claiming the complete day pack is ready.

Share only information the receiving facilitator needs. Separate restricted support or welfare notes from learner-facing materials, and preserve the user's intended recipients. Do not transfer a whole student record into a public worksheet or shared prompt for convenience.

Check the handoff from the recipient's position: can they find materials, start the activity, adapt if access fails, assess the result and return the work without contacting the absent author? Identify any dependency that still prevents this.

## Draft a learner report from supplied evidence

Separate observations, achievement judgments and proposed next steps. If asked to turn an educator's judgments into prose, retain those judgments instead of silently replacing them with inferred grades. If actual assessment is requested, use the supplied rubric and evidence in the assessment workflow; a report-writing request alone is not that instruction.

Use the requested audience, tone and length. Ground each claimed strength or difficulty in supplied work or observations; connect the next step to that evidence. Do not invent a personal anecdote, motivation, diagnosis or improvement trend. When evidence is sparse, narrow the claim or flag the gap rather than fill it with flattering boilerplate.

Use the institution's supplied scale and current standard when alignment is requested. Do not assume letter grades, a curriculum vendor, or that a standard defines every grade boundary. State unverified alignment plainly. Style examples are optional and never a prerequisite to a useful draft.

For batches, keep stable learner identifiers and check that evidence and comments have not crossed rows. Resolve conflicting names, pronouns, judgments or observations before treating a comment as ready. Remove unnecessary identifiers from intermediate review material where practical.

Before delivery, check each comment for an evidence-grounded observation and a practical next step tied to that learner; do not replace the next step with a disclaimer. Return the requested drafts plus concise unresolved factual checks for the responsible educator. Producing a draft is not publishing it, assigning a formal grade, or proving learning occurred.
