# Venture falsification and planning

Use this entrypoint before committing meaningful time, money, or organizational attention to a venture thesis. It converts uncertainty into decisions and experiments; it does not provide investment, legal, tax, or financial advice.

## Build the evidence ledger

State the thesis, current stage, target user or buyer, proposed value, business mechanism, time horizon, and available resources. Separate facts, derived observations, assumptions, forecasts, opinions, and unknowns. Give each source a date and scope. If current market or customer evidence is missing, make that absence a first-class result rather than filling it with generic market size.

Rank existential assumptions by impact, cost to test, reversibility, dependency, and time sensitivity. Distinguish a belief that can be falsified from a preference that can only be chosen. Assign a decision owner and a next review date to every assumption that can block the stage.

## Design a staged test

1. Choose the smallest experiment that can change the decision. Define who or what is observed, the artifact or intervention, the sample or exposure boundary, and the evidence that counts.
2. Precommit primary and guardrail metrics, minimum evidence, threshold, time window, resource cap, and stop rule. Include null, underpowered, contradictory, and instrumentation-failure outcomes.
3. Keep discovery, prototype, pilot, and scale decisions separate. A successful conversation, mockup, or synthetic estimate can support the next test without proving demand or unit economics.
4. Record the result with source links, deviations, exclusions, and uncertainty. Update the assumption status without rewriting the original prediction.
5. Choose continue, revise, pause, or kill. If the result is inconclusive, name the cheapest evidence that would resolve it; do not call ambiguity momentum.

If the authorized task includes building or running the experiment, execute only the declared scope and preserve receipts. Customer contact, paid acquisition, data access, or financial commitment require their own explicit authority and privacy treatment.

## Finish and limits

Return a thesis map, assumption ledger, ranked risks, experiment cards, resource and kill criteria, decision record, and unresolved evidence gaps. Finish when every existential assumption is supported, bounded for testing, or explicitly accepted as uncertainty with an owner. Forecasts remain forecasts until matched to observed evidence.

## Common failure modes

- Treating enthusiasm or a polished deck as demand evidence.
- Testing a low-risk detail while the existential assumption remains unexamined.
- Changing the success threshold after seeing results.
- Conflating a prototype pass with a scalable operation.
- Spending or contacting people because the plan sounds persuasive.
