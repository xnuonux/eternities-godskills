# Jurisdiction-aware contract review

Produce a clear, source-linked issue list from an agreement. Support the user's review and preparation for counsel; do not represent the analysis as a lawyer's advice or a conclusion about enforceability.

## Establish the document and objective

Identify the parties, document version, attachments, referenced policies, governing-law clause, effective date, signature state, and the user's position and goals. Distinguish missing attachments from absent obligations. Ask for jurisdiction only when it affects a requested legal conclusion; basic extraction can proceed with that uncertainty visible.

Read the entire supplied agreement before prioritizing isolated clauses. Follow definitions, precedence rules, exceptions and cross-references. A heading may not describe the legal effect of the text beneath it.

## Build an obligation map

For each material obligation, record actor, action, trigger, deadline, evidence of performance, exception and consequence. Separate mandatory language from discretion, conditions and statements of intent.

Compare the user's intended transaction with provisions concerning payment, scope and acceptance, term and renewal, termination, confidentiality, data handling, intellectual property, warranties, liability, indemnity, dispute resolution, and change control. Include only relevant topics; a small agreement does not require an encyclopedia.

Look for cross-clause conflicts: an unlimited indemnity beside a liability cap, a deletion duty beside a retention clause, or acceptance language that changes payment timing. Calculate dates from an explicit assumed effective date and calendar convention, and label assumptions.

## Distinguish three kinds of finding

- **Document fact:** quote a short necessary excerpt or precise clause locator and explain it in plain language.
- **Business tradeoff:** show how the clause compares with the user's stated objective and propose a negotiation question or alternative wording.
- **Legal question:** identify the jurisdiction-dependent issue. Verify any cited rule or case in current authoritative material and seek qualified review where stakes warrant it. If verification is unavailable, leave it as a question rather than inventing a citation.

Do not classify a clause as illegal, unenforceable or standard solely because it sounds unusual. A template from another jurisdiction is not legal authority.

## Deliver and verify

Return a concise issue table with clause location, observation, practical consequence, uncertainty, suggested question and priority. If asked for a redline, preserve the original and make changes inspectable; explain substantive changes instead of silently rewriting the agreement.

Check quotations, defined terms, cross-references, dates and internal consistency. Keep confidential documents within the authorized processing boundary. Do not sign, accept terms, send a negotiation message, or submit a filing merely because a review was requested. Completion means the user can see the issues and their evidentiary basis—not that the agreement has been legally approved.
