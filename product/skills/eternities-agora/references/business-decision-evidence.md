# Business decision evidence

Use this method when an account, opportunity, service issue, or evidence room combines records that support different conclusions. The goal is a decision-ready packet, not a universal customer score or a compulsory CRM workflow. Select the relevant evidence lanes; do not manufacture five sections for a one-question task.

## Establish the decision and its evidence lanes

Name the decision, accountable owner, represented identities, audience, date boundary, and any supplied decision rule. Retain each source's date and scope. Separate observed facts, derived values, proposed actions, analyst assessments, and unresolved items.

- **Documents:** track requirement, availability, freshness, access restriction, validation, and approval independently. A file can be present, stale, restricted, and unvalidated at the same time. Presence is not truth, current applicability, or permission to share. Count required and optional documents separately; expose each denominator and eligibility rule rather than announcing a single misleading readiness percentage.
- **Pipeline:** compare dated buyer or seller evidence with supplied stage entry and exit criteria. Preserve the recorded stage separately from the evidence-supported assessment. Missing exit evidence calls for a next check, not a fabricated buyer commitment, forecast probability, or automatic stage advancement.
- **Account health:** keep usage, support, relationship, and outcome signals separately attributable. Increased adoption and an unresolved serious issue can both be true. Do not average them into a reassuring score unless an explicit, appropriate scoring rule exists.
- **Metrics:** preserve definition, period, cohort, baseline, unit, and source. Distinguish calculation from interpretation; a changed denominator or tracking rule can defeat comparability. State which additional observation could reverse the interpretation.
- **Escalation:** describe impact, timeline, reproduction or documentary evidence, work already attempted, unknowns, and the requested decision. Apply an official severity only when its policy basis is known. A clearly labeled analyst risk assessment can still guide prompt attention; lack of a policy is not a reason to ignore harm.

## Close the decision without erasing uncertainty

Show the evidence supporting and opposing the leading option. Distinguish a blocker to the requested action from a gap that does not affect it. Offer proportionate options such as proceed within a defined scope, hold the affected action, request a specific fact, or escalate to a named role. Give the next action, owner or unassigned state, timing if supplied, and the condition for reconsideration. Keep a proposed owner or deadline labeled as proposed.

When several decisions share a packet, retain a separate gate for each. For example, advancing a recorded stage and distributing a document bundle can have different prerequisites. Do not silently add the bundle's requirements to the stage's supplied rule, or treat one approval as satisfying the other. State any proposed stricter rule as a recommendation, not an existing criterion. When summarizing document completeness, give the required-item numerator and denominator explicitly alongside the independent validation and access states; list optional items separately.

Separate internal decision notes from any requested customer-facing draft. Minimize confidential information; do not attach restricted records merely because their existence was counted. No analysis establishes a refund, renewal, fix date, legal conclusion, or authorization to mutate accounts.

Verify calculations and trace the decisive claims before handoff. Continue downstream work already authorized by the user when its prerequisites are satisfied; this method neither grants new authority nor requires asking again for existing authority. Report actual effects separately from recommendations. No specific vendor, paid API, local directory, or organizational policy is required.
